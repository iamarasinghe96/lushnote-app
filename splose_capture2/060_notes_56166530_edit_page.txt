Subjective




John reports a significant improvement in his morning energy levels following the school’s approval of a designated parking bay closer to the administration building.

Leisure: Successfully played 4 holes of golf on Sunday. He felt tired afterward but noted that his left-hand grip remained stable thanks to the specialized glove.

IADLs: Used the one-handed pasta drainer successfully; reports feeling confident enough to handle "boiling water tasks" without supervision now.




Objective

He successfully navigated the rolling seat along the garden path, though he required a reminder to engage his core when leaning forward to prevent balance loss.

Safety (Hot Tasks): Observed a simulation of draining a pot using the new AT. John demonstrated a safe "slide and pour" technique, minimizing the need to lift a heavy, hot pot with his weaker left hand.

Fatigue Management: John's "Pacing Diary" shows he has successfully integrated a "horizontal rest" (lying down for 15 minutes) immediately upon returning home from work, which he says "recharges the battery" for evening family time.




Assessment




John is reaching a "maintenance phase" for several of his primary goals.

Goal 4 (Golf): Endurance is the final hurdle. He is managing 4 holes well; the next step is building the stamina for a full 9-hole round.




Plan

Gardening: Trial a "ratchet-style" pruner to reduce the force required for the left hand during garden maintenance.

Workplace: Follow-up email to school HR to formally close out the workplace accommodation request for the parking bay.

Self-Management: John to continue the 15-minute post-work rest and monitor wrist discomfort during gardening, limiting pruning sessions to 15-minute intervals.

Reason for referral




John has been referred for Occupational Therapy (OT) to support his ongoing community reintegration and workplace maintenance. Following a stroke several years ago, John has successfully returned to his high-level role as an Assistant Deputy Principal. The focus of this NDIS-funded intervention is to address persistent left-sided upper limb weakness and fatigue. Specific areas of focus include energy conservation for workplace longevity, prescription of low-cost assistive technology (AT) for home/work, and facilitating engagement in gardening, cooking, and golf.




Subjective history




John reports that while he is "glad to be back at the chalkface," his 45-hour work week is physically and mentally taxing. He notes significant "3 PM fatigue," where his left arm feels heavy and his ability to process complex scheduling tasks slows down.

Work: Manages staff rosters and student discipline. Finds long walks across the school campus tiring.

Home: John enjoys cooking but currently relies on his wife for heavy lifting or bilateral tasks (e.g., chopping veg, draining pasta). He misses gardening but finds his current tools difficult to grip.

Leisure: He has recently returned to the local golf club but struggles with grip strength on his left side, affecting his swing and confidence.




Medical history




Primary Diagnosis: Right Ischaemic CVA (2022) resulting in Left Hemiparesis.

Secondary Conditions: Hypertension (managed), Mild Sleep Apnoea.

Cognition: Reported minor deficits in executive functioning (specifically multi-tasking under pressure), though neuropsychological testing indicates these are within functional limits for his current role.

Physical: Left-sided neglect has resolved; however, fine motor coordination in the left hand remains impaired.




Social history




Living Situation: Lives in a single-story 4-bedroom house with his wife and one adult son.

Support System: Strong emotional support from family and colleagues.

Environment: Large suburban block with a vegetable patch. School workplace involves multiple buildings with some stairs (ramps available).

Transport: Independent driver (modified vehicle with steering spinner knob).




Client goals




Workplace: "To manage my energy levels so I’m not exhausted by Thursday, allowing me to continue working full-time."

IADLs: "To safely prepare a three-course meal independently using adaptive equipment."

Leisure (Gardening): "To maintain my vegetable garden for at least 60 minutes without hand pain."

Leisure (Golf): "To improve my left-hand grip enough to play 9 holes of golf comfortably."




Objective assessment




Upper Limb (Left): Full passive range of motion (PROM) in the shoulder; however, active range (AROM) is limited to 110° flexion.

Grip Strength: Measured at 12kg on the left (compared to 42kg on the dominant right side).

Fine Motor Coordination: Significant impairment noted during manipulation of small items, such as buttons and coins, using the left hand.

Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS): John scored 5.2/7, which indicates that fatigue has a significant impact on his daily functioning.

Cognition (MoCA): Scored 27/30. Observations during the Trail Making Test (Part B) showed minor delays, suggesting slight deficits in executive function and task-switching.

Balance & Mobility: Generally independent, though a slight circumduction gait becomes visible during late-afternoon sessions as fatigue increases.




Clinical impression




John presents as a highly motivated individual who has made an impressive vocational recovery. His primary barriers are neurological fatigue and reduced left-hand dexterity. While his cognitive "fog" is minor, it is exacerbated by physical exhaustion. Without energy conservation strategies, he is at risk of burnout or workplace injury. His left-sided weakness currently limits his participation in high-dexterity hobbies (gardening/golf) and increases his dependence on his spouse for domestic chores.




Management plan




1. Energy Conservation & Fatigue Management (Work/Home)

Implement "Pacing" education: Breaking the school day into high/low energy blocks.

Trial of a "perching stool" for use in the school office and kitchen to reduce standing tolerance requirements.

Cognitive strategy training (e.g., use of digital checklists to mitigate minor executive function lapses).

2. Assistive Technology (Low-Cost)

Kitchen: Prescription of a one-handed workstation (spiked board), rocker knife, and "stay-warm" plates.

Gardening: Trial of ergonomic, long-handled tools with "easy-grip" adaptations and a rolling garden seat.

Golf: Research and trial of a specialized golf glove with integrated grip-aid/strapping for the left hand.

3. Functional Task Training

Home visits to practice meal preparation using the new AT.

Workplace environmental assessment to optimize office ergonomics and reduce unnecessary walking distances.

4. Review

Progress review scheduled for 8 weeks to evaluate the efficacy of the fatigue management plan and AT integration.

Subjective




John reports a "noticable shift" in his energy levels since implementing the perching stool in his office and kitchen.

Workplace: Reports that using the stool during student discipline meetings has reduced his leg heaviness by 4:00 PM. He has started using a "Focus Mode" on his computer for 30 minutes after lunch to manage cognitive fatigue.

Home: John successfully used the spiked chopping board and rocker knife to prepare a stir-fry. He noted he felt "less like a bystander" in the kitchen.

Leisure: He has visited the driving range twice using the new specialized grip glove. He reports he can now maintain a firm hold on the club throughout the swing without the left hand "slipping."

Concerns: He still finds the walk from the school car park to his office (approx. 250m) exhausting on Monday mornings.




Objective




Grip Strength (L): Re-measured at 15kg (increase of 3kg from initial assessment). This improvement appears linked to increased functional use of the hand during gardening and golf.

AT Review (Kitchen): Observed John using the rocker knife. He demonstrated safe technique and required minimal verbal cues for positioning. He is now 100% independent with vegetable preparation.

Energy Conservation: John presented a "Pacing Diary" he kept for the last 7 days. He successfully identified three "high-fatigue" triggers (long staff meetings, yard duty, and heavy gardening) and has begun scheduling 15-minute "rest breaks" immediately following these tasks.

Cognition: Observed John using his school’s digital calendar to set reminders for "uninterrupted admin time." He navigated the software without error or frustration.




Assessment




John is making excellent progress toward his NDIS goals, particularly regarding Goal 1 (Workplace) and Goal 2 (IADLs).

The implementation of low-cost AT in the kitchen has successfully reduced his reliance on his wife for meal prep.

The specialized golf glove has provided the necessary mechanical support to allow him to re-engage in his primary leisure pursuit, which is also serving as an incidental strengthening exercise for his left upper limb.

Fatigue is still a limiting factor during high-mobility tasks (walking across campus), suggesting a need to review his workplace environmental transit.




Plan




Next Session: Workplace site visit (scheduled for 27/05/2026) to assess the walking path from the car park. We will investigate the possibility of an allocated "Accessible" parking bay closer to the administration building or the use of a lightweight transit solution.

Kitchen Goal: Move from vegetable prep to "hot task" safety, trialing a one-handed pasta drainer/pot insert.

Gardening: Trial "easy-grip" handle extensions on his existing pruning shears to see if they further reduce hand fatigue.

Home Exercise: Continue with functional hand use through golf and gardening (30–45 minute sessions, twice weekly).
