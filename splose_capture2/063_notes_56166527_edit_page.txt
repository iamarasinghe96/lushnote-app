Reason for referral




Jane is a 48-year-old female referred by her GP following a recent diagnosis of Multiple Sclerosis (MS) by a neurologist. As a newly accepted NDIS participant, she seeks physiotherapy to manage her symptoms, maintain her physical capacity for her high-demand career, and continue her participation in community sports (pickleball).




Subjective history




Jane reports a recent onset of intermittent sensory changes and mild fatigue. She describes herself as "determined" to not let the diagnosis sideline her career or active lifestyle.

Current Symptoms: Occasional "heaviness" in the lower limbs after long days at work and mild balance instability during fast-paced lateral movements (specifically noted during pickleball).

Fatigue: Reports 4/10 on average, peaking in the late afternoon.

Vision/Cognition: No significant deficits reported at this time.




Medical history




Multiple Sclerosis: Recently diagnosed; currently under neurologist review for Disease Modifying Therapy (DMT) initiation.

Hypertension: Well-managed via GP-prescribed medication.

Type 2 Diabetes: Well-managed through diet and medication; regular BSL monitoring.

Other: No recent hospitalisations or surgeries.




Social history




Occupation: Project Manager in Industrial Engineering (Full-time, hybrid office/site visits).

Home Environment: Lives in a two-story home with her husband. Two children currently reside at home while attending university.

Leisure: Active pickleball player (2-3 times per week).

Support: Strong family support network.




Client goals




Work Longevity: To continue working full-time in her engineering role for the foreseeable future.

Physical Activity: To remain playing competitive pickleball without increased falls risk or excessive post-exercise fatigue.

Self-Management: To develop a "flare-up" plan and understand energy conservation techniques.




Objective assessment




5 Timed Sit to Stand (5TSTS): 9.5 seconds. * Normative Reference (Ages 40–49): ~7.4 seconds (± 1.6s).

Interpretation: Jane is currently performing in the lower quartile for her age group, suggesting early-stage deficits in lower limb explosive power.

Berg Balance Scale (BBS): 51/56. * Normative Reference (Ages 40–49): 54–56.

Interpretation: Minimal falls risk, but notable deficits in tandem stance and single-leg standing (R < L). These deficits correlate with her reported "clumsiness" during lateral movements on the pickleball court.

12-Item MS Walking Scale (MSWS-12): 18/60. * Interpretation: This baseline score indicates that MS is not yet significantly impacting her functional walking, though she perceives slight limitations when navigating uneven terrain or hills.

Fatigue Severity Scale (FSS): 4.2/7. * Interpretation: Scores > 4.0 are generally considered to indicate clinically significant fatigue. Jane is just crossing this threshold, making proactive energy conservation education a priority.

Gait Analysis: Jane demonstrates an independent and fluid gait. There is a subtle reduction in left arm swing when walking speed is increased (stress gait), but no evidence of foot drop or Trendelenburg sign at this stage.

Strength (Manual Muscle Testing): Generally 5/5 throughout. However, a slight "fade" to 4+/5 was noted in right hip flexion and ankle dorsiflexion after repetitive testing, indicating some early neurogenic fatiguability.

Sensation: Reports slight "fuzziness" to light touch in a stocking distribution (distal to ankles) bilaterally; proprioception at the great toe remains intact.




Clinical impression




Jane is in the early stages of navigating her MS diagnosis. She presents with high physical baseline function but exhibits "sub-clinical" deficits in balance and lower-limb power compared to her age-matched peers. Her primary risks are fatigue-related burnout and potential injury during high-intensity sports (pickleball) if balance is not optimized. Her well-managed comorbidities (HBP/Diabetes) require continued monitoring during aerobic conditioning.




Management plan




Exercise Program Design (16 hours): Development and implementation of a neuro-protective exercise program focusing on strength, balance and cardiovascular activity adapted for MS, and lateral stability drills for pickleball.

Fatigue Management & Education (2 hours): Development and implementation "pacing" strategies for her project management role and heat-sensitivity education.

Review and Reporting (2 hours): Non face to face time for MDT and report writing.
